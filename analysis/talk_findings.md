### 5b. Why exact_gp fails on shared eval — multi-modality + lengthscale collapse

The function Ωh²(pMSSM parameters) is genuinely multi-modal: it's a sum over physically distinct annihilation channels, each dominant in a different region of parameter space, each with its own characteristic lengthscale. A single global RBF kernel cannot express this.

**Concrete physical example — the bino/higgsino transition near μ ≈ M_1**:

The 19 pMSSM parameters include three masses that control the lightest-neutralino composition: `M_1` (bino), `M_2` (wino), `mu` (higgsino).

- |μ| ≫ M_1 ⇒ LSP is **pure bino**: weak SM-coupling, annihilates inefficiently, **Ωh² is HIGH** (often overclosing, > 0.5)
- |μ| ≈ M_1 ⇒ LSP is a **well-tempered bino-higgsino mix**: mass-dependent, Ωh² ≈ 0.12 (the constraint band)
- |μ| ≪ M_1 ⇒ LSP is **pure higgsino**: strongly coupled to Z, W, h; annihilates efficiently, **Ωh² is LOW** (often < 0.01)

The transition can happen over Δμ ~ 5-20 GeV, while μ itself ranges over ~3 TeV in the pMSSM scan. So Ωh² has a sharp ~100× change over a few-percent range in μ. Equivalent statements apply to the bino/wino transition (M_1 vs M_2), the chargino co-annihilation channel (M_2 ≈ μ), and to several others:

- **Z-pole resonance** at m_LSP ≈ M_Z/2 (~45 GeV) — sharp spike when 2·m_bino crosses the Z mass
- **Higgs funnel** at m_LSP ≈ m_h/2 (~62.5 GeV) — narrow s-channel enhancement via SM Higgs
- **A-funnel** at 2·m_LSP ≈ m_A — particularly important at large tanβ; the (m_A, tanβ) lengthscale is highly non-stationary
- **Stau co-annihilation strip** when m_stau ≈ m_LSP (within ~10%) — drops Ωh² by 1–2 orders of magnitude

Each of these is a different "mode" of the function. **No single set of ARD lengthscales can describe all of them well**.

**The data confirms this**: exact_gp's learned ARD lengthscales shrink dramatically over AL iterations on exactly the multi-modal axes:

| param | iter 1 | iter 5 | iter 20 | iter 40 | shrink factor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M_1 (bino) | 0.14 | 0.25 | 0.085 | **0.046** | 3.0× |
| M_2 (wino) | 0.18 | 0.08 | 0.068 | **0.055** | 3.3× |
| mu (higgsino) | 0.26 | 0.094 | 0.078 | **0.069** | 3.8× |
| tanb | 0.13 | 21 | 15 | 3.5 | volatile |

Meanwhile `M_3` (gluino, decoupled from neutralino physics) stays pinned to its upper bound 1.0, and `mA` similarly saturates — confirming the kernel optimiser correctly identifies the *physically relevant* axes but then over-tightens them in response to AL-concentrated data.

The mechanism:
1. Multi-modality means a single global RBF is misspecified — fine when training data is roughly uniform (Baseline), broken when concentrated near transition lines (AL).
2. AL's entropy-batch selects exactly the boundary points where these multiple modes overlap.
3. The GP's hyperparameter optimiser responds by shrinking lengthscales — its only knob to "fit harder" with a stationary kernel.
4. Short lengthscales → memorise individual training points → train R² stays high while val R² and shared-eval R² collapse.

This is the cleanest example I've seen in the data of **AL biasing the training distribution into a regime that activates a latent model-misspecification problem**. It does not happen with deep_gp because deep kernel learning lets effective lengthscales vary across input space — the misspecification is absent. For the talk, this is a perfect specific-mechanism slide: "AL is not metric-neutral; it interacts with the surrogate's expressivity in ways that depend on the underlying physics."
